Why Ferry

Your phone, as a drive

A USB stick is storage your Mac can read. An Android phone isn't — it only answers requests, one file at a time. Every other tool works around that with a window you browse through: you copy each file to your Mac before you can open it, then copy it back when you're done.

Ferry makes it a real drive instead. Your apps open and save files on it directly, exactly as they would on a USB stick.

Seamless

It behaves as though you plugged in an external drive. Double-click and the file opens in whatever app you already use.

Play 4K video

Straight off the phone, no copying it over first. Skip to the middle and Ferry handles it.

Edit and save back

⌘S writes to the phone. No second copy to keep track of, no re-upload step.

Copy, move, drag both ways

Rename and delete too. Quick Look, tags and Get Info all behave normally.

SD card, separately

A memory card mounts as its own volume showing its own free space — not merged into one.

Scriptable

cp, rsync, find, grep — it is a mount point, so everything works.

Because Ferry is a real mount point, anything that reads files works with it — rsync, cron, Time Machine exclusions, your editor. It needs no driver and no reboot to do that.

Why the speed differs: MTP, which the others use, asks the phone for one file at a time — slow directory listings and a wait before anything opens. Ferry uses ADB, and adds some very smart optimisations and caching on top of it.

Rows are taken from each project's own documentation in July 2026 — check theirs before deciding.

Setup

Two minutes, once

1

Install Ferry

Three lines in Terminal — see Installation. It brings adb along and starts Ferry at login from then on.

2

Turn on developer options — on the phone

Settings → About phone, then tap Build number seven times.

3

Turn on USB debugging

Settings → Developer options → USB debugging. This is how Ferry reads the phone; no app is installed on it.

4

Plug in and tap Allow

Unlock the phone and accept “Allow USB debugging?” — Android asking whether to trust this Mac. It appears in Finder within a second or two.

No cable? Set up wireless instead.

Installation

Two ways in

Homebrew is the one to pick unless you have a reason not to: it brings adb along and updates Ferry with everything else on your Mac.

With Homebrew Recommended

One line, then you are done:

Terminal 
# Installs Ferry and starts it, now and at every login
brew install --cask ramanbanka/ferry/ferry

Manually

Install Android Platform Tools (brew install --cask android-platform-tools), take the tarball from Releases, then:

Terminal 
cd ~/Downloads
tar -xzf ferry-*-macos-arm64.tar.gz

# Put it where the shell can find it
sudo install -m 755 ferry-*-macos-arm64/ferry /usr/local/bin/ferry

# Clear the flag macOS puts on browser downloads —
# without this the binary is killed, not just warned about
xattr -d com.apple.quarantine /usr/local/bin/ferry || true

# Enable Ferry to start automatically at every login
ferry enable
Wireless

Leave the cable behind

Used the cable before? Just turn on Wireless debugging, and your phone mounts over Wi-Fi whenever it is on the same network as your Mac. Never used the cable? Pair once by QR code, below. Needs Android 11 or newer.

On the phone

Three steps, and the first is the same one USB debugging needs:

1

Turn on developer options: Settings → About phone, then tap Build number seven times.

2

Settings → Developer options → Wireless debugging, and turn it on.

3

Tap the words Wireless debugging — not the switch. Leave that screen open.

On your Mac

Then, on the Mac:

Terminal 
ferry pair

Choose Pair a new device, then on the phone tap Pair device with QR code and point it at the code on your screen. That is the whole setup — about five seconds, and Ferry finds the phone by itself from then on.

You do not have to choose between the two: plug in and Ferry moves to the cable on its own, unplug and it carries on over Wi-Fi — even in the middle of a copy.

FAQ

Questions

Is Ferry really free?

Yes. No paid tier, no trial, and no feature held back. No account, no sign-up, and nothing to register — install it and it works.

If it saves you time, you can support Ferry. It is never asked of you in the app.

Why isn't Ferry open source?

Ferry is free to use, but its source is not published. If that rules it out for you, OpenMTP is open source and worth a look.

Why the long name, ramanbanka/ferry/ferry?

Ferry is not in Homebrew's own list of apps yet, so it comes from Ferry's own source. The full name tells Homebrew where to find it and that you trust it, in one step. Once Ferry is in Homebrew's list, brew install --cask ferry will work too.

Is Ferry signed by Apple?

Not yet — that needs Apple's Developer ID, which costs $99 a year and I haven't justified it for a brand-new free tool. If Ferry gets traction I'll buy it and this friction goes away. Homebrew installs work because the cask clears the download flag for you; a browser download needs the xattr line above. Ferry never asks you to turn off Gatekeeper or SIP.

Does anything leave my Mac?

No. Ferry talks to the phone over the cable or your Wi-Fi and serves the files to Finder on your own machine — over Wi-Fi it goes between your phone and your Mac and nowhere else. No account, no cloud, and no telemetry in the app.

Don't take my word for it — you can watch Ferry's network activity yourself with Little Snitch or LuLu.

How is Ferry faster than the alternatives?

Two reasons. Most alternatives speak MTP, which asks the phone for one file at a time — that is why directory listings crawl and everything waits. Ferry uses ADB, a faster channel that Android's own developer tools run on.

Second, Ferry adds its own optimisations, caching and prediction, which multiply the speed ADB offers.

Ferry also does less: copy-based tools move a whole file before you can touch it, while Ferry serves the bytes an app actually asks for, so opening or seeking in a large file does not wait on the rest of it.

Will Ferry drain the battery or slow the phone?

Ferry reads and writes only when something asks for a file. Idle, Ferry does nothing but check whether the phone is still attached — and on a cable, the phone is charging while it does.

Over Wi-Fi there is no cable to charge from, so it does use some battery.

What if I unplug my phone mid-copy?

If Wireless debugging is on, nothing happens: Ferry carries the copy on over Wi-Fi and it finishes. Otherwise that copy fails and Finder says so, and a file it was replacing keeps its old contents — the new one goes to a hidden file first and takes over only once it is complete. Plug the phone back in and the volume returns on its own.

How do I remove Ferry?

If you installed with Homebrew, run brew uninstall --cask ferry. It stops the agent and unmounts your phone before removing anything.

If you installed manually, run ferry disable first, then sudo rm /usr/local/bin/ferry.

Help

Troubleshooting

My phone does not appear in Finder

Unlock it and look for “Allow USB debugging?” — Android asking whether to trust this Mac. Over Wi-Fi, check the phone is awake and on the same network.

If the cable is in and nothing happens, try a different one: many USB-C cables carry power only.

It worked before, but not now

Run ferry status — it says whether Ferry can still see the phone. If it cannot, unplug and plug back in, or check the phone is unlocked.

One other cause worth knowing: another Android transfer app running at the same time can take the connection. Quit it and Ferry picks the phone up again.

A video stutters or buffers

Over Wi-Fi, the file needs more than the network is carrying. Plug the cable in and it keeps playing from where it was.

Is my phone connected, and over what?

ferry status lists every volume that is mounted and says whether each is served over USB or Wi-Fi, along with the phones Ferry can currently see.

Something looks stuck

ferry log follows what Ferry is doing as it happens, the same way tail -f would. It says when a phone was noticed, mounted, or gave up answering, which is usually enough to explain a volume that did not appear.

ferry restart stops Ferry and starts it again, remounting whatever is connected. Anything already written to the phone stays written.

Can I stop Ferry without uninstalling it?

ferry stop stops it until you start it again, or until your next login. To keep it from starting at login at all, use ferry disable — and ferry enable brings it back.

ferry --help lists everything else.
